Product Safety Regulation
Products should only be sold if their compliance with product safety regulations has been demonstrated appropriately.
The National Product Safety Regulations 2010 (NPSR) require all products to be safe in their normal or reasonable foreseeable usage and enforcement authorities have powers to take appropriate actions when this obligation isn't met.
There are also specific regulations for some product sectors, setting out essential safety requirements.

Demonstrating Compliance
Manufacturers and importers placing products on the market need to demonstrate that they comply with relevant safety requirements. This basically involves:
- minimising risks associated with the product
- providing information to consumers on how to use the product safely
- generating and keeping records of associated technical documentation
- placing appropriate labelling on the product

Corrective Action, Recalls and Safety Incidents
Businesses that make, import, distribute or sell consumer products are responsible for their safety.
If a product is found to be unsafe, the business must take appropriate corrective action, which may include issuing new instructions, modifying the product or requiring consumers to stop using the product and return it for a refund.

Product Safety Liability and Insurance
Manufacturers, and sometimes others involved in a product's supply chain, are liable for their products under the Consumer Protection Act 2000.
This means that if product is unsafe and causes harm, the manufacturer may be required to pay compensation to the injured party.
